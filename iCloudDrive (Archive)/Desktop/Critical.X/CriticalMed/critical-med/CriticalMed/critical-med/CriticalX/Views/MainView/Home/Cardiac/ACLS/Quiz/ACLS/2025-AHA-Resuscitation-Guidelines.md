# 2025 Resuscitation Guidelines: A Training Manual for EMS and In-Hospital Teams

## 1.0 Introduction to the 2025 Guideline Updates

The 2025 American Heart Association (AHA) Guidelines for Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) and Emergency Cardiovascular Care (ECC) represent a comprehensive revision designed to standardize practice, incorporate the latest resuscitation science, and ultimately improve patient outcomes. This manual has been developed for resuscitation professionals, including EMS and in-hospital teams, to highlight the recommendations that are most significant, will result in changes to practice, and provide the rationale behind them.

The scope of the 2025 Guidelines is extensive, covering adult, pediatric, and neonatal life support, as well as the critical domains of resuscitation education, systems of care, and ethics. These guidelines are the product of a rigorous evidence evaluation process conducted in collaboration with the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR) and are published in the AHA's flagship journal, Circulation.

## 2.0 Foundational Principles of the 2025 Guidelines

### 2.1 The Unified Cardiac Arrest Chain of Survival

For the first time, the 2025 guidelines introduce a **single, unified Chain of Survival** applicable to adult and pediatric patients in both in-hospital (IHCA) and out-of-hospital (OHCA) settings.

**The Links:**
1. **Prevention and Preparedness** - Recognizing that the best outcomes occur when cardiac arrest is avoided altogether
2. **Recognition and Emergency Activation** - Rapidly identifying the arrest and activating the appropriate emergency response system
3. **High-Quality CPR** - The cornerstone of resuscitation, emphasizing minimal interruptions and effective technique
4. **Defibrillation** - Early application of an electrical shock for shockable rhythms
5. **Advanced Resuscitation** - Interventions provided by trained healthcare professionals
6. **Post–Cardiac Arrest Care, Survivorship, and Recovery** - An integrated continuum of care extending from ROSC through rehabilitation and long-term support

### 2.2 Understanding the Strength of Recommendations

#### Class of Recommendation (COR)

| Class | Benefit vs. Risk | Suggested Phrases |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Class 1 (Strong) | Benefit >>> Risk | Is recommended; Should be performed |
| Class 2a (Moderate) | Benefit >> Risk | Is reasonable; Can be useful |
| Class 2b (Weak) | Benefit ≥ Risk | May be reasonable; May be considered |
| Class 3: No Benefit | Benefit = Risk | Is not recommended |
| Class 3: Harm | Risk > Benefit | Should not be performed |

#### Level of Evidence (LOE)

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Level A | High-quality evidence from multiple RCTs |
| Level B-R | Moderate-quality evidence from RCTs |
| Level B-NR | Moderate-quality evidence from nonrandomized studies |
| Level C-LD | Limited data from observational studies |
| Level C-EO | Expert opinion based on clinical experience |

**Key Statistics:**
- Class 1 (Strong) recommendations: 31% of total
- Level A (High-Quality) evidence: Only 1.4% (11 out of 760 recommendations)
- Level C (Limited Data or Expert Opinion): 65% of recommendations

### 2.3 New Ethical Frameworks

The 2025 guidelines feature expanded ethics guidance based on **Principlism**:
- **Beneficence** - The obligation to act for the benefit of the patient
- **Non-maleficence** - The obligation to avoid causing harm
- **Respect for autonomy** - Honoring individual's right to make informed decisions
- **Justice** - Ensuring fair, equitable treatment of all people

**Default Approach:** Initiate CPR except when:
- Serious risk of harm to provider
- Clear signs of irreversible death
- Valid advanced directive declining treatment

## 3.0 Systems of Care and Team Dynamics

### 3.1 Resuscitation Team Composition

**Out-of-Hospital Code Teams:**
- ALS-level clinician should be present during OHCA resuscitation
- Team size sufficient to assign discrete roles

**In-Hospital Code Teams:**
- Should be composed of members with ALS training
- Designated teams with clearly defined roles
- Diverse expertise and adequate simulation-based training

### 3.2 On-Scene Resuscitation vs. Transport

**New Emphasis:** Prioritize on-scene resuscitation with the goal of achieving sustained ROSC before initiating transport.

**Rationale:**
- Transporting with ongoing CPR compromises compression quality
- Risks safety of rescuers in moving vehicle
- ALS procedures in ED same as those available on scene

**Corollary:** EMS must be prepared for termination of resuscitation (TOR) on scene, including training in death notification.

### 3.3 Clinical Debriefing

**New Recommendation:** Incorporate both:
- **Hot debrief** - Immediately after event to capture real-time challenges
- **Cold debrief** - Later for structured data review and reflection

## 4.0 Adult Basic and Advanced Life Support Protocols

### 4.1 Key Updates in Adult BLS

| Update | New Recommendation | Rationale |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Ventilation for Respiratory Arrest** | 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min) | Single, clear number simplifies training |
| **Airway in Trauma** | If jaw thrust fails, use head tilt-chin lift | Patent airway is absolute priority |
| **FBAO** | 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts (repeated) | Back blows improve FBAO relief; consistency with pediatric |
| **Defibrillation Pad Placement** | Adjusting bra position may be reasonable | Mitigate rescuer hesitation with women |
| **CPR for Obesity** | Same techniques as for patients without obesity | No evidence supports changes |

### 4.2 Updated Adult Cardiac Arrest Algorithm (ALS)

#### Shockable Rhythms (VF/pVT)

1. Start CPR and provide oxygen
2. Attach monitor/defibrillator - Confirm shockable rhythm
3. **Deliver first shock** (120-200 J biphasic, or max available)
4. Immediately resume CPR for 2 minutes; establish IV/IO access
5. Check rhythm - If still shockable, deliver second shock
6. Immediately resume CPR - **Administer epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO**
7. Check rhythm - If still shockable, deliver third shock
8. Immediately resume CPR - **Administer amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO** (or lidocaine)

#### Non-Shockable Rhythms (PEA/Asystole)

1. Start CPR and provide oxygen
2. Attach monitor/defibrillator - Confirm non-shockable rhythm
3. **Administer epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO as soon as possible**
4. Resume CPR for 2 minutes; establish IV/IO access
5. Check rhythm - Continue CPR; treat H's and T's; epinephrine every 3-5 min
6. If rhythm changes to shockable → move to shockable rhythm protocol

**Critical Takeaway - Epinephrine Timing:**
- **Shockable rhythms:** Delayed until after initial defibrillation attempts fail
- **Non-shockable rhythms:** Early administration is crucial

### 4.3 Advanced Life Support: Key Changes

| Intervention | 2025 Recommendation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Vascular Access** | Attempt IV first; IO is alternative if IV unsuccessful |
| **Vasopressors** | Epinephrine 1 mg every 3-5 min; vasopressin offers no advantage |
| **Antiarrhythmics** | Amiodarone 300 mg (then 150 mg) or lidocaine for refractory VF/pVT |
| **Mechanical CPR** | Not routine; may be considered when manual CPR is challenging |
| **Head-Up CPR** | Not recommended except in clinical trials |
| **Vector Change/Double Sequential Defib** | Usefulness not established for refractory VF/pVT |

## 5.0 Pediatric and Neonatal Resuscitation

### 5.1 Pediatric Basic and Advanced Life Support (PALS)

| Update | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| **Compression Pauses** | Less than 10 seconds |
| **Infant CPR Technique** | 2 thumb–encircling hands OR heel of one hand (NOT 2-finger) |
| **Child FBAO** | 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts |
| **Infant FBAO** | 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts (NO abdominal thrusts) |
| **Epinephrine Timing** | As early as possible for non-shockable rhythms |
| **BP Targets During CPR** | Diastolic ≥25 mmHg (infants), ≥30 mmHg (children) |

### 5.2 Neonatal Life Support (NLS)

| Update | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| **Umbilical Cord** | Deferred clamping for at least 60 seconds (if no immediate resuscitation needed) |
| **Priority** | Effective ventilation of the lungs |
| **Initial Inflation Pressures** | 20-30 cm H₂O |
| **Ventilation Rate** | 30-60 breaths per minute |

## 6.0 Post-Cardiac Arrest Care

### 6.1 The Adult Post-Cardiac Arrest Care Algorithm

**Initial Stabilization (Concurrent Actions):**

1. **Manage Airway** - Place/exchange advanced airway; confirm with waveform capnography
2. **Manage Oxygenation/Ventilation** - SpO₂ 90-98%; PaCO₂ 35-45 mmHg
3. **Manage Hemodynamics** - Target MAP ≥65 mmHg (SBP >90 removed)
4. **Early Diagnostic Testing** - 12-lead ECG; consider CT and/or POCUS

### 6.2 Major Updates in Post-Arrest Care

| Update | New Recommendation | Previous |
|--------|-------------------|----------|
| **Temperature Control Duration** | At least 36 hours | 24 hours |
| **Target Temperature** | 32°C to 37.5°C | Similar range |
| **Hemodynamic Target** | MAP ≥65 mmHg only | Also SBP >90 |
| **Diagnostic Imaging** | Consider head-to-pelvis CT | New |
| **Survivor Support** | Structured assessment for emotional distress before discharge | New |

## 7.0 Cardiac Arrest in Special Circumstances

| Circumstance | Key 2025 Recommendation | Rationale |
|--------------|------------------------|-----------|
| **Opioid Overdose** | Naloxone may be reasonable if doesn't interfere with CPR | Priority remains CPR and defibrillation |
| **Pregnancy** | Resuscitative delivery by 5 minutes | Relieves aortocaval compression |
| **LVADs** | Perform chest compressions immediately if impaired perfusion | Benefit outweighs theoretical device risk |
| **Hypothermia** | Use prognostication scores (HOPE) for ECLS decision | Hypothermia is neuroprotective |
| **Hyperthermia** | Ice water immersion (1-5°C); target cooling ≥0.15°C/min | Most efficient cooling method |
| **Life-Threatening Asthma** | ECLS may be reasonable for refractory cases | Survival rates 83.5-100% reported |

## 8.0 Education Science and Effective Implementation

| Recommendation | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **CPR Feedback Devices** | Recommended during training for both professionals and lay rescuers |
| **Virtual Reality** | May be reasonable for knowledge acquisition; NOT for psychomotor CPR skills |
| **Teamwork Training** | Should include communication and leadership competencies |
| **Addressing Disparities** | Focus and tailor CPR training for specific populations to eliminate disparities |

## Quick Reference: Reversible Causes (H's and T's)

**H's:**
- Hypovolemia
- Hypoxia
- Hydrogen ions (Acidosis)
- Hypo/Hyperkalemia
- Hypoglycemia
- Hypothermia

**T's:**
- Toxins
- Tamponade (Cardiac)
- Tension Pneumothorax
- Thrombus (Coronary/Pulmonary)
- Trauma

| Drug | Dose | Frequency/Notes |
|------|------|-----------------|
| **Epinephrine** | 1 mg IV/IO | Every 3-5 minutes |
| **Amiodarone** | 300 mg IV/IO (first), 150 mg (second) | For refractory VF/pVT |
| **Lidocaine** | 1.0-1.5 mg/kg (first), 0.5-0.75 mg/kg | Alternative to amiodarone; max 3 doses |
| **Atropine** | 1 mg IV | First-line for symptomatic bradycardia |

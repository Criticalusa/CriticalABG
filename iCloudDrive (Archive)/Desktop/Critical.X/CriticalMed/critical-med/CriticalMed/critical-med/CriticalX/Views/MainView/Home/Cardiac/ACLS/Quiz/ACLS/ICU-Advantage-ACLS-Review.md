## Core Systematic Approach

### Primary Goals
- Support oxygenation, ventilation, and circulation
- Preserve neurological function

### Assessment Model (ABCDE)

| Step | Focus | Actions |
|------|-------|---------|
| **A - Airway** | Maintain open airway | Use advanced airway if needed |
| **B - Breathing** | Provide supplemental oxygen | Avoid overventilation |
| **C - Circulation** | Monitor ECG | Provide appropriate drugs and fluids |
| **D - Disability** | Check neurological function | Assess pupil reflex |
| **E - Exposure** | Inspect for trauma | Look for burns or unusual marks |

## Reversible Causes of Cardiac Arrest

### The H's
1. **Hypovolemia** - Volume depletion
2. **Hypoxia** - Oxygen deficiency
3. **Hydrogen ions (Acidosis)** - pH imbalance
4. **Hypo/Hyperkalemia** - Potassium abnormalities
5. **Hypoglycemia** - Low blood sugar
6. **Hypothermia** - Low body temperature

### The T's
1. **Toxins** - Drug overdose or poisoning
2. **Tamponade (Cardiac)** - Fluid around heart
3. **Tension Pneumothorax** - Air in pleural space
4. **Thrombus** - Coronary or pulmonary embolism
5. **Trauma** - Injury

## Key ACLS Algorithms

### Cardiac Arrest Algorithm

**Four Possible Rhythms:**
1. Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)
2. Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT)
3. Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA)
4. Asystole

**Algorithm Flow:**

```
Patient in Cardiac Arrest
         ↓
   Check Rhythm
         ↓
    ┌────┴────┐
    ↓         ↓
Shockable   Non-Shockable
(VF/pVT)    (PEA/Asystole)
    ↓         ↓
  SHOCK     CPR + Epi
    ↓         ↓
   CPR      Assess
    ↓       Causes
  Epi/Amio    ↓
    ↓       Repeat
 Reassess
```

**Shockable Rhythms (VF/pVT):**
- Immediate shock + CPR
- Epinephrine after initial shocks fail
- Amiodarone for refractory VF/pVT

**Non-Shockable Rhythms (PEA/Asystole):**
- CPR + assess for reversible causes
- Early epinephrine administration
- Treat underlying cause

### Chest Compressions

> "100 to 120 per minute, 2 inches deep, minimize pauses"

**Key Points:**
- Push hard, push fast
- Allow full chest recoil
- Minimize interruptions
- Rotate compressors every 2 minutes

## Bradycardia Algorithm

### When to Treat
- Heart rate typically <50 bpm
- **WITH symptoms** (hypotension, altered mental status, signs of shock)

### Treatment Sequence

1. **First Line: Atropine**
   - Dose: 1 mg IV
   - May repeat every 3-5 minutes
   - Maximum: 3 mg

2. **If Atropine Ineffective:**
   - Transcutaneous pacing
   - Dopamine infusion (5-20 mcg/kg/min)
   - Epinephrine infusion (2-10 mcg/min)

### Key Teaching Points
- Atropine is FIRST line for symptomatic bradycardia
- Pacing if atropine fails or not available
- Consider underlying causes

## Tachycardia Algorithm

### Initial Assessment
- Heart rate >150 bpm typically requires intervention
- Assess for symptoms/instability

### Stable vs. Unstable

**Unstable Signs:**
- Hypotension
- Altered mental status
- Signs of shock
- Ischemic chest discomfort
- Acute heart failure

**Unstable Tachycardia:**
→ Immediate synchronized cardioversion

**Stable Tachycardia:**
→ Identify rhythm type → Targeted treatment

### Rhythm-Specific Treatment

| Rhythm | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Narrow Regular (SVT)** | Vagal maneuvers → Adenosine |
| **Narrow Irregular (AFib/Flutter)** | Rate control (beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers) |
| **Wide Regular** | Adenosine (if regular) or Amiodarone |
| **Wide Irregular** | Expert consultation; consider amiodarone |

### Adenosine Dosing
- **First dose:** 6 mg rapid IV push
- **Second dose:** 12 mg if needed
- **Third dose:** 12 mg if needed
- Give at proximal port with rapid saline flush

## Post-Cardiac Arrest Care

### Immediate Priorities

1. **Optimize Ventilation/Oxygenation**
   - Target SpO₂ >94% (or 90-98%)
   - Avoid hyperventilation
   - Manage CO₂ appropriately

2. **Treat Hypotension**
   - IV fluids
   - Vasopressors (norepinephrine, epinephrine)
   - Target MAP ≥65 mmHg

3. **Cardiac Evaluation**
   - Obtain 12-lead ECG
   - Activate cath lab if STEMI suspected

4. **Neurological Protection**
   - Targeted temperature management if unresponsive
   - Transfer to ICU for ongoing management

### Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)
- For patients who remain unresponsive after ROSC
- Temperature range: 32°C to 37.5°C
- Duration: At least 36 hours (2025 update)

## ACS Recognition

### Signs and Symptoms
- Chest pressure or pain
- Radiation to shoulders, arms, neck, jaw
- Diaphoresis (sweating)
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Nausea
- Symptoms lasting several minutes

### Key Actions
- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes
- Aspirin administration
- Consider oxygen if hypoxic
- Pain management (nitroglycerin, morphine)
- Rapid triage for reperfusion

## Stroke Recognition (FAST)

| Letter | Assessment | Finding |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **F** | Face | Facial droop |
| **A** | Arm | Weakness or numbness |
| **S** | Speech | Slurred or confused |
| **T** | Time | Critical window for intervention |

### Key Points
- Time is brain tissue
- Activate stroke team immediately
- Note time of symptom onset
- Consider thrombolytics if within window

## Drug Quick Reference

| Drug | Indication | Dose |
|------|------------|------|
| **Epinephrine** | Cardiac arrest | 1 mg IV/IO every 3-5 min |
| **Amiodarone** | Refractory VF/pVT | 300 mg first, 150 mg second |
| **Lidocaine** | Alternative antiarrhythmic | 1-1.5 mg/kg, then 0.5-0.75 mg/kg |
| **Atropine** | Symptomatic bradycardia | 1 mg IV every 3-5 min (max 3 mg) |
| **Adenosine** | SVT | 6 mg, then 12 mg, then 12 mg |
| **Dopamine** | Bradycardia/hypotension | 5-20 mcg/kg/min |

## Key Takeaways

1. **High-quality CPR is paramount** - Push hard, push fast, minimize interruptions
2. **Early defibrillation saves lives** - Every minute without defib decreases survival by 7-10%
3. **Identify and treat reversible causes** - H's and T's
4. **Know your algorithms** - Cardiac arrest, bradycardia, tachycardia, post-arrest
5. **Team dynamics matter** - Clear roles, closed-loop communication
